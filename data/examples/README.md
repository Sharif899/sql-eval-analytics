# Example data

Synthetic, original data for `sqleval`. Nothing here is real: no client
data, no real annotators, no real model outputs. It was generated by a
one-off script (seed 42; the script was deleted after generation, the CSVs
are the deliverable). Dates are January–February 2026.

## Files

| File | Rows | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `annotators.csv` | 8 | Annotators, by cohort (3 junior, 2 mid, 3 senior). |
| `items.csv` | 60 | Annotation items: 20 yoruba-qa (factual_qa), 20 maths-tutor (maths), 20 safety-review (10 safety, 10 instruction_following). |
| `annotations.csv` | 122 | Two judgements per item (120), plus 2 deliberate duplicates. |
| `reviews.csv` | 116 | QA review of 116 annotations; 6 annotations have no review (the backlog). |
| `eval_runs.csv` | 2 | model-a and model-b, one run each over `eval-items-v1`. |
| `scores.csv` | 120 | Every item scored by both models (60 × 2). |

## Deliberate flaws

Each one is planted so that a specific query exposes it. The numbers
quoted are the actual `sqleval report` output on this data, not targets.

1. **Duplicate submissions.** `itm-003` was submitted twice by A03 (same
   label, 5 hours apart); `itm-006` was submitted twice by A06 (label
   changed, 26 hours apart). `duplicate_submissions` finds both.
2. **A slow annotator.** Funke (A06, junior) submits 72–140 hours after an
   item is created; everyone else submits within 4–30 hours. Her lag shows
   in `annotator_throughput` (she is the only annotator still submitting in
   the week of 2026-02-16) and in the submission timestamps.
3. **An ambiguous project.** Safety-review agreement is 75.0% (κ 0.502),
   against 85.0% (κ 0.721) for maths-tutor and 80.0% overall (κ 0.643).
   The guidelines for safety-review are the ones that need rewriting, not
   the annotators.
4. **Overconfident juniors.** Juniors report the highest confidence
   (A03 0.90, A06 0.88, vs A01 0.79 and A02 0.80 for the seniors) and
   disagree with their partners more often. A06 has the largest
   overconfidence gap: 0.88 confidence against 73.3% agreement (gap 0.15).
5. **A slow reviewer.** Bola (A02) averages 116.9 hours per review with
   100% of reviews over 48 hours; the other reviewers average 17.3 hours
   with 0% over 48 hours. `review_turnaround` makes the bottleneck
   unmissable.
6. **A review backlog.** 6 annotations have no review row; the oldest
   unreviewed annotation was submitted 2026-02-14 02:00:00.
   `reviewer_workload` reports the backlog on every row.
7. **A bad batch.** Maths-tutor reviews in the third data week were
   rejected at 35% (vs 12% elsewhere). It shows as a 28.6% reject rate for
   maths-tutor juniors, against 0.0% for maths-tutor seniors — the batch
   hit the juniors hardest.
8. **A miscalibrated model.** model-b scores itself 3.0–3.4/5 on maths
   while getting only 37–42% of the facts right (calibration gap 0.18–0.27,
   pass rate 20–40%). Its best rubric scores are on instruction following
   (4.6–5.0), where it beats model-a — the one category it wins, and even
   there its factual accuracy (0.69–0.73) lags its rubric.
